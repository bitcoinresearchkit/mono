      How it reads

      1. The long-term cloud: how holders feel

        The 40th and the 60th percentile of the price paid, by dollar, by coins held over 4 months, over 5 months and over 6 months: six lines close together, the cloud between them.

        That's the compass's north and south.

      2. The short-term cloud: how recent buyers feel

        The same for coins held under 4 months, under 5 months and under 6 months: what recent buyers paid.

        That's east and west. Price is each day's close.

      3. Three trends set the territory

        For the same three short-term groups, a line on the lowest price any of their coins was bought at, a support, until price touches it; then on the highest, a resistance, until price touches that.

        When all three hold supports, it's bull territory; all three resistances, bear; split, limbo.

      Every line here is by dollars and counts mostly the coins that move (each age group weighted by how likely its coins are ever to be spent), so lost coins don't hold a cloud down. Every state is read from that day's numbers alone, never from what came after: today's can change by tonight's close.
