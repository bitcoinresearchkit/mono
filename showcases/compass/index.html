      How it reads

      1. The long-term cloud sets the cycle

        The median price paid by coins held over 4 months, over 150 days and over 6 months: three lines close together, the cloud between them.

        Above it is a cycle's bull territory; below it, its bear territory; inside, a transition. That's the compass's north and south.

      2. The short-term cloud sets the moment

        The same for coins held under 4 months, under 150 days and under 6 months: what recent buyers paid.

        Above it, the moment is bullish; below it, bearish; inside, undecided. That's east and west. Price is each day's close.

      3. Three trends set the direction

        For the same three short-term groups, a line on the lowest price any of their coins was bought at, a support, until price touches it; then on the highest, a resistance, until price touches that.

        When all three hold supports, the trend is bull; all three resistances, bear; split, it's in limbo.

      A median here is by dollars and leaves out coins unlikely ever to move (each age group weighted by how likely its coins are to be spent), so lost coins don't hold a cloud down. Every state is read from that day's numbers alone, never from what came after: today's can change by tonight's close.
