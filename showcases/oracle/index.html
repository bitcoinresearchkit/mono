01

Round dollars leave a fingerprint

People pay $10, $20, $100, rarely $13.70. In sats, those round amounts sit at the same distances from one another whatever the price: a fingerprint of 19 teeth, from $1 to $10,000.

Below are the payments it counted at a block, by amount: now, or any block of the week. Drag the fingerprint across them: where its teeth all land on spikes, that's the price. Elsewhere it fits in part at best, most at double or half the price, where $50 payments pass for $100 ones.

02

Again, every block

Each new block, it counts again: its payments and those of the 11 before, the newest weighing most.

    newest block12th

    And it looks only near its last answer, never more than about 16% higher or 13% lower, so never as far as the echoes at double or half the price. One odd block can't throw it far; the line moves a block at a time.

    03

    It skips what isn't a purchase

    Only outputs that look like someone paying someone count.

    • The miner's reward–New coins, not a payment.
    • Payouts to many–More than 250 outputs: an exchange or a pool paying out.
    • Transactions with data–Any with an OP_RETURN output: records, not purchases.
    • Taproot outputs–Mostly protocol traffic, which drowns the signal.
    • Dust–Under 1,000 sats: too small to be a payment.
    • Round bitcoin amounts–0.01 BTC, 50,000 sats: round in bitcoin, so not in dollars.

    Each output counts under the first rule that skips it, in this order: a whole transaction goes first, then its outputs one by one. The bars are shares of all the outputs.
