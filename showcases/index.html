Bitcoin's on-chain data, open to all

Bitview reads every block from a Bitcoin node and measures it: prices, supply, holders, miners, fees and much more. Chart it, query it, or run the server yourself. Free, with no account and no key.

Ways in

The same data, however you work with it.

Studio→

Chart any series: overlay them, stack panes, derive new ones with formulas, then keep, share or export them. Presets to start from, on your phone as on your desk.

API→

Every series, block, transaction and address over plain HTTP, documented from the server's own specification.

GET /api/series/price/day1/latest
OpenAPIllms.txtJavaScriptPythonRust

For assistants↗

Add Bitview to Claude, ChatGPT or any MCP client: it searches the series by their names and descriptions, and reads them.

https://mcp.bitview.space/

Run your own→

The same server beside your own Bitcoin Core, on Linux or macOS. Open source, under the MIT license.

cargo install --locked bitviewd

Explore

Pages built on the same data.

Dollar-cost averaging→

The same dollars of bitcoin every day, week or month: what any plan since 2010 would be worth, beside the same plan buying more when bitcoin was cheap against what holders paid, and how every start and length turned out.

Explorer→

Every series Bitview computes, folder by folder: what each one is, its history drawn, and how to fetch it.

Globe→

Where blocks are mined, by their pools' home countries: live, over any period, and month by month since 2011.

Calendar→

Every day of the year, the pool that found the most blocks, and every block of any day.

The Satoshi Trail→

Supply by the price it last moved at, through time, in two or three dimensions.

Underneath

What Bitview is made of, open for anyone to build on.

Bitcoin Research Kit→

The Rust crates Bitview is built from: RPC, block files, mempool, prices, types and storage.
