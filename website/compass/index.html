      The index

      Where the day sits on the compass.

      0 is the compass’s Fear corner, 100 its Optimism corner, and each level one of its diagonals, as the trends are; both clouds count the same.

      Time at each value

      Two-point buckets include their lower edge; the last includes 100. Cumulative shares count time below a bucket's lower edge, each day counting half as much every four years back. The days counted run up to the day shown above, as known that day. Use the arrow keys, Home and End to read buckets. Escape returns to the day shown.

      How it’s computed

      1. The clouds

        Every coin was last bought at some price: what it cost its holder. Above what they paid, holders are in profit; below it, at a loss. The compass’s rows are the long-term cloud, the price against what coins held over 4, over 5 and over 6 months cost, three age groups; its columns are the short-term cloud, coins held under 4, under 5 and under 6 months.

        Each cloud is drawn from its three groups: the prices 40% and 60% of their dollars were bought under, and their average cost. Coins that never move, lost ones among them, would drag it down, so each group counts mostly its coins that move: each group weighted by how likely its coins are ever to be spent.

      2. The nine moods

        Each day sits in one of the compass’s nine cells, by where the price is against each cloud: above it, inside or below.

        The nine fall into three trends along the diagonals. Each side counts +1 above its cloud, 0 inside and −1 below; added, they give the trend: Bull above zero, Mixed at zero, Bear below.

      3. Each day

        Price is each day’s close. Every reading is made from that day’s numbers alone, never from what came after: today’s can change by tonight’s close.

        A day is extreme when the price is further above or below either cloud than on 99 in 100 days before it, each cloud ranked against its own earlier days alone, recent years counting more (each day half as much every four years back), from a year after the first day: about 2.2% of days with the clouds the page opens on, 1.9 to 2.5% with the others. It follows the coins and the clouds chosen at the page’s foot. Its mood is called extreme over the chart and in the panel by the compass, and its colour is drawn stronger in the chart’s fill, the pan bar and the strip under the compass; the lines keep theirs.

        A run is a mood’s days in a row. A mood’s median run, in the panel by the compass, is the middle length of its runs ended by the day shown, recent ones counting more: half are shorter, half longer.
