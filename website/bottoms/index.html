Each four-year cycle has one bottom: the low of its deepest fall.

A cycle runs between two halvings, the cuts to new-coin issuance that come about every four years. A fall runs from an all-time high to the lowest close before the next one, and counts when the price has at least halved.

    A line's miss is how much further the price fell after reaching it.

      What the names mean.

      Most lines are an average of the prices at which coins last moved, as the realized price is, or a percentile of those prices. Bedrock floors and rarity lines are Bitview's own.

      Bedrock 99% floor
      The price at which holders' losses would be as heavy as in the worst 1% of Bitcoin's history. A 98% floor is the worst 2%, and so on.
      Awake and mobile
      Give more weight to coins that still move than to coins long idle. Awake counts each coin by how much of its age band's holding time has been spent, mobile by how likely its age band is ever to be spent. Unweighted counts every coin alike.
      20th percentile realized price
      The price under which a fifth of the coins last moved. A capitalized price counts dollars instead of coins: a fifth of the money paid for them.
      99% rarity line
      The price at which Bitcoin would be as cheap, against the line it is named after, as on its cheapest 1 day in 100 since late 2012. Recent years count for more.
      Long-term holders
      Holders of coins that haven't moved for 150 days or more.

      How a line is scored.

      1. Each day's price is held against the line as it stood the evening before, since some lines move on the day the price reaches them.
      2. A line is met where it stood. If it had risen above the price, it is met at the previous day's close instead.
      3. Lines with the same worst miss are ordered by their next worst.
      4. Bottoms count from 2017, on daily closing prices.
