Each four-year cycle has one bottom: the low of its deepest fall.

A cycle runs between two halvings, the cuts to new-coin issuance that come about every four years. A fall runs from an all-time high to the lowest close before the next one, and counts when the price has at least halved.

    A line's miss is how much further the price fell after reaching it.

      Every line, closest first.

      Bitview makes most of the lines here: Bedrock, the rarity lines, and some of the prices they rest on. It made Bedrock and the rarity lines in 2026, after these bottoms: their place here describes the past, not a tested forecast. Every other line names where it comes from; those of 2019 were made after the first bottom here.

        What the names mean.

        Most lines are an average of the prices at which coins last moved, as the realized price is. Two kinds are Bitview's own.

        Bedrock 99% floor
        The price at which holders' losses would be as heavy as in the worst 1% of Bitcoin's history. A 98% floor is the worst 2%, and so on. Awake and mobile give more weight to coins that still move than to coins long idle, each in its own way. Unweighted counts every coin alike.
        99% rarity line
        The price at which Bitcoin would be as cheap, against the line it is named after, as on its cheapest 1 day in 100 since late 2012. Recent years count for more.

        How a line is scored.

        1. Each day's price is held against the line as it stood the evening before, since some lines move on the day the price reaches them.
        2. A line is met where it stood. If it had risen above the price, it is met at the previous day's close instead.
        3. Lines with the same worst miss are ordered by their next worst.
        4. Bottoms count from 2017, on daily closing prices. Ranked the same way on daily lows, Bedrock awake 99% floor also came first (checked Nov 26, 2026).
        5. The power-law floor's constants were fitted in 2019, with the Dec 2018 bottom in the data. Refitted on the closes before that fall, its miss there would be 41.6%, not 31.6%.
        6. The median realized prices name no one: Glassnode publishes the same measure, and who was first isn't established.
