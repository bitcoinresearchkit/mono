Every coin has a price: Bitcoin's price when it last moved.

When today's price is above it, the coin is in profit. Below it, at a loss.

Lower the price, and fewer of the coins in play are in profit.

Bedrock finds the price where they'd be so few that it has only happened on about 5 days in 100. Then 2 in 100, then 1, down to 1 in 1,000. Those five prices are its floors.

It takes four steps. Here is each one, with today's numbers.

Step 1 of 4 · Weigh

Not every coin is in play.

Millions of coins haven't moved in ten years or more. A coin like that is unlikely to be sold tomorrow, so it shouldn't count like one that moved last week.

The coins are sorted by age, the time since each last moved, and every age gets a weight between 0 and 1: 1 counts a coin fully, 0 not at all.

In the picture, each bar is an age. Its height is the coins of that age, and its lit part is how much they count.

Weight

The Awake weight builds on the Cointime framework. It looks back: for each age, take every coin that was ever that old and see what share has moved since. Coins that stayed that old for longer count for more. The other choice, Mobile, comes from Coinflow, a Bitview model, and looks ahead, to the chance that a coin that old ever moves again. The Mobile weight comes from Coinflow, a Bitview model. It looks ahead: for each age, the estimated chance that a coin that old ever moves again, judged from how fast coins of each age have moved so far. The other choice, Awake, builds on the Cointime framework and looks back, at the share of coins that old that have moved since.

With either weight, a coin that just moved counts almost fully, and one that hasn't moved in fifteen years counts for almost nothing.

A common shortcut is to leave out every coin unmoved for over seven or ten years. But a cutoff is a cliff: a coin a day short of it counts in full, one a day past it not at all, so the choice of cutoff decides whether the – BTC aged seven to ten years count. Old coins do wake up: in 2025, 312,000 BTC over seven years old moved, 130,000 of them over ten. And a cutoff counts every younger coin in full, lost ones too. Neither weight has a cliff: each age counts by how often coins that old have moved, so no age is all in or all out.

The whole page follows the weight you pick. The weight is borrowed; the ranking and the floors that follow are Bedrock's own.

Step 4 of 4 · Price it

Lower the price until profit gets that rare.

Take the stack from step 2 and lower its line. As the price falls, fewer coins are in profit. Each time the share reaches one of the five marks, the price that got it there is a floor.

The awake coins that last moved above the first floor are split by price into ten slices, each with the same number of coins. The five higher slices are water, the five lower sand.

Every day

Done again each day, the floors draw the rock.

Coins move and each day adds one more to rank against, so the five floors are worked out again every day. Day after day, they make the rock in the landscape at the top of this page. Each day is ranked only against the days before it, never with hindsight.

The ranking uses every day since 2009, but this page's counts start in 2015. Before that the history to rank against is short and prices come from exchange records, so the charts mark those years "Calibrating": drag one back to see them.

Since 2015 the price has been under the first floor in a handful of visits.

The reasoning

When most awake coins are at a loss, few sellers should be left.

Selling a coin below the price it last moved at means taking a loss. At the first floor, about three in four of the awake coins are at a loss, and more at each floor below. Most holders who could sell would be selling at a loss, and those willing to do that have likely done so already. With few sellers left, it would take little buying to stop the fall.

That is the reasoning. The record above is the evidence, and it is short.

Why the weights matter

Count every coin the same and the picture blurs. Coins that haven't moved in a decade last moved at a sliver of today's price. They are in profit at almost any price, and almost none of them move.

What it can't tell

  • The record is short. A few visits to the rock can't prove a rule.
  • A floor isn't a wall. It marks where profit would be that rare, not where the price must stop.
  • The floors move. They are worked out again each day, so today's floors aren't next month's.
  • A rank isn't a chance. "Only 1% of earlier days had fewer in profit" describes the past. It doesn't say there's a 1% chance of going lower.
  • The last move isn't always a sale. A coin moved between its owner's own wallets takes a new price here.
  • The weights are estimates. Nobody knows which coins will move. An age's weight is a judgement from how coins of that age behaved before.

Every number is computed by this Bitview server from Bitcoin's public record; prices before late January 2015 come from exchange records. None of this is advice to buy or sell.
